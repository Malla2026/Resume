excerpt: 'This study examines energy system development and its associated greenhouse gas and local air pollutant emissions under base case and three different emissions reduction targets in Thailand during 2000-2035. Base case results show that fossil fuels would continue to dominate the total energy supply and the corresponding CO2 emissions would increase by more than four-folds between years 2000 and 2035. Power-, transportation- and industry- sector together would contribute 93% of total CO2 emissions by 2035. With limited availability of economically viable new and renewable energy sources and indigenous fossil fuels, the country’s energy import dependency under the base case would reach 
77% in 2035 from 50% in 2000. With ER targets set from 2013, the results of the study show a fall in coal share and an increase in natural gas share in the total primary energy-mix during 2013-2035. However, the new and renewable 
energy sources would play a limited role in primary energy-mix. The power sector would play a higher role in reducing CO2 emissions than the other sectors especially at high ER targets. Under ER15, up to 41% of SO2 and 10% of NOx emissions could be reduced as co-benefits. New technologies such as carbon capture and storage, and solar for electricity generation; and hydrogen based fuel cell- and hybrid- vehicles for road transportation would become cost-effective under ER targets. The incremental CO2 mitigation cost would vary from 36 US$ per t-CO2 under ER target of 5% to 49 US$ per t-CO2 under ER target of 15% during 2013-2035.'
