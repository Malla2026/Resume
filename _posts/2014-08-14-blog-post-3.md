Nepal government is aggressively promoting development of long distance East-West and North-South electric railways, and Kathmandu Metro railway network systems in the country. The development of substantial railway transportation system is anticipated over the coming 20 years. In July 2013, Nepal government requested South Korea for assistance in developing national railway network in the country.

Nepal is a landlocked country of 26.5 million (2011 census) people with China to the North and India to the South. Nepal's area of 147181 km2 is roughly 1.5 times the area of South Korea. Geographically, Nepal is divided into three distinct east-west ecological regions: mountains (the Himalayas) in the north, tarai (the plains) in the south, and hills in between. As of 2012, Nepal's per capita GDP (constant 2005 PPP US$) is 1279 which is less than 5% (27991) of South Korea. During the same year, Nepal’s primary energy use and electricity consumption in per capita terms are 381 kgoe and 144 kWh, respectively. For comparison, these figures represent only 7.4% of primary energy use and 1.5% of electricity consumption of average South Korean. 

Diplomatic relations between these two countries is established in 1969 at the Counselor level. In 1974, South Korea established its embassy in Kathmandu and Nepal established its embassy in Seoul in 2007. South Korea is one of the key development partners for Nepal. Since 1991, the South Korean government is contributing to the development of Nepal through various programs. South Korea is also one of the five top investors in Nepal. As of July 2012, there are 171 projects in operation in Nepal under South Korean investment with an estimated value of NRs 6422 million (US$ 73.7 million). These projects are responsible for creating 6820 jobs in the country.

South Korea is also the top seven labor destination country with the highest number of Nepalese migrant workers. In 2004, South Korean government introduced Employment Permit System (EPS) that allows Nepalese migrant workers to go to work in South Korea. The EPS has made it possible for thousands of young Nepalese migrant workers to find fair and equal employment opportunities in South Korea. Since the start of air services between these two countries, the number of South Korean tourists increased dramatically, from 12,913 in 2006 to 26,004 in 2012.

Recently, Nepal government has requested expression of interest for consulting services from international and local firms for the feasibility study of three major railway projects. The feasibility study is expected to cover wide range of issues including detailed analysis on existing transport facilities, engineering requirements, demand for usage of the railway lines, economic and financial analysis, resettlement and land use and environmental planning. The study also requires assessment of project’s viability on economic cost-benefit evaluation including environmental and socioeconomic criteria. 

The consortium of companies for preparing the feasibility study for Mechi-Mahakali electrified railway and Kathmandu MRT railway include Chungsuk Engineering, Kunhwa Consulting and Engineering, Korean Transport Institute and Korea Rail Network Authority (KRNA) from South Korea, and Intercontinental Consultants and Technocrats, Shah Consult International, Full Bright Consultancy, Environment and Resource Management Consultant and Building Design Authority from Nepal. 

Looking forward, there are possibilities of works in many areas including engineering design, economic and environmental impact assessment, and travel demand modeling and forecasting in Nepal's railway development. For many South Korean private and public companies, it is also an opportunity to do business and demonstrate their expertise in Nepal. 

The main purpose of this scoping paper is to provide brief background information on Nepal's transport systems, mainly railways, development. This paper also highlights the possibilities, challenges and opportunities of doing business for South Korean agencies that have interest in Nepal's transport systems development.
