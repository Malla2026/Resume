Fuelwood is the largest energy source in Nepal and more than half of all households use it as a primary energy source for their cooking needs. Despite many benefits of clean cooking – cooking with cleaner fuels and energy-efficient modern cookstoves –, the quantity of fuelwood consumed by Nepalese households has not decreased in proportion with their rising income. To overcome this, the government has initiated several plans and received overseas development assistance in finding solutions for clean cooking, yet it remains elusive. Only 0.5% of households currently use electricity for cooking and LPG is concentrated in urban households. Biogas is location-specific and limited to cattle-owning households. For many low-income households, these cleaner fuels are prohibitively expensive for cooking compared to fuelwood which is available either for free or at a very low cost. I explore the challenges of clean cooking in Nepal and identifies possible solutions to address households’ heavy reliance on fuelwood.
